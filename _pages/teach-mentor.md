## Google's Astronomical Influence Campaign
As part of the requirements for the Intensive Education Studies Certificate, I drafted a report on the Google's involvement in space and cloud industries and how that affects astronomers. I come to the conclusion that Google, intentionally or not, has ingratiated itself in astronomy research through cloud computing resources and direct funding, thereby lending the company an amount of power over astronomy research. Such a dynamic has previously resulted in the [suppresion of policy research critical of Google](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/08/30/us/politics/eric-schmidt-google-new-america.html).

You can [view the submitted draft here](/assets/pdfs/AstroxGoogle_v4.pdf). NOTE: As school projects go, I still consider this document rather rough --- there are instances here, as my advisors have pointed out, where I am not as convincing as I'd like to be. Nonetheless, I believe that the document is useful insofar as it lists Google's interests in the space industry and how that impacts or may impact astronomers.

## Splash @ Yale
I have taught several 1-day courses through [Splash @ Yale](https://yale.learningu.org/). Typically, I walk students through simple concepts in astronomy (e.g. photometry, spectroscopy) and use think-pair-share and group discussions to get them to apply these concepts to real-world problems (e.g. Tabby's Star, water plumes on Europa). I end with a discussion of planetary defense and ethics. [Here is a sample slide deck](/assets/ppts/sprout_f25.pptx).
